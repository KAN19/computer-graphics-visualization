Core Concept
1. What is Camera Viewpoint & Transformation?
• World Coordinates: Fixed global reference frame with origin at (0, 0, 0). 3D objects reside at absolute positions.
• Camera Coordinates: Observer-centric frame where origin is the camera eye position $\mathbf{camPos}$. The 2D screen cares only about whether an object lies to the Left / Right ($X_{\text{cam}}$), Up / Down ($Y_{\text{cam}}$), and Depth distance in front ($Z_{\text{cam}}$).
• Viewpoint Transformation: Translates the camera to the origin (0, 0, 0) and rotates the frame to align with the camera's sightline.
Step 1 — Linear Algebra
2. Compute Camera Orthonormal Basis Vectors
From 3 inputs: camera position $\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{camPos}$, target point $\mathbf{t} = \mathbf{target}$, and reference world up vector $\mathbf{up} = (0, 0, 1)$, we construct 3 mutually orthogonal unit vectors ($\mathbf{f}, \mathbf{r}, \mathbf{u}$):
$$ \mathbf{f} = \frac{\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{c}}{\|\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{c}\|}, \qquad \mathbf{r} = \frac{\mathbf{f} \times \mathbf{up}}{\|\mathbf{f} \times \mathbf{up}\|}, \qquad \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{f} $$
Live Calculation Breakdown:
// A. Forward axis (line of sight from camera to target)
dir = target - camPos
= (0 - (20), 0 - (-20), 0 - (20))
= (-20.00, 20.00, -20.00)
length = √((-20)² + 20² + (-20)²) = 34.64
Forward = dir / length = (-0.58, 0.58, -0.58)
// B. Right axis — Right = normalize(Forward × up)
Right_raw.x = Forward.y * up.z - Forward.z * up.y = 0.58
Right_raw.y = Forward.z * up.x - Forward.x * up.z = 0.58
Right_raw.z = Forward.x * up.y - Forward.y * up.x = 0.00
Right = Right_raw / length = (0.71, 0.71, 0.00)
// C. True camera Up axis — TrueUp = cross(Right, Forward)
TrueUp = (-0.41, 0.41, 0.82)
🎯 The 3 camera axes form an Orthonormal Basis (all unit length, mutually 90° orthogonal).
Step 2 — Coordinate Projection
3. Transform Point P from World Space to Camera Space
For any 3D world point P = (7.5, 7.5, 7.5), we translate relative to the camera origin, then project onto the camera's orthonormal axes using the Dot Product:
$$ \mathbf{rel} = \mathbf{P} - \mathbf{camPos}, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} X_{\text{cam}} \\ Y_{\text{cam}} \\ Z_{\text{cam}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{rel} \cdot \mathbf{r} \\ \mathbf{rel} \cdot \mathbf{u} \\ \mathbf{rel} \cdot \mathbf{f} \end{bmatrix} $$
Live Calculation Breakdown:
// 1. Translation: relative vector from camera to point: rel = P - camPos
rel.x = 7.5 - (20.0) = -12.50
rel.y = 7.5 - (-20.0) = 27.50
rel.z = 7.5 - (20.0) = -12.50
// 2. Projection onto axes via Dot Product:
X_cam = rel · Right
= (-12.5 * 0.71) + (27.5 * 0.71) + (0)
= 10.61 (Right of camera lens)
Y_cam = rel · TrueUp
= (-12.5 * -0.41) + (27.5 * 0.41) + (-12.5 * 0.82)
= 6.12 (Above camera lens)
Z_cam = rel · Forward (Depth along line of sight)
= (-12.5 * -0.58) + (27.5 * 0.58) + (-12.5 * -0.58)
= 30.31 (In FRONT of camera, depth 30.31)
💡 Coordinates in Camera Space: (X_cam: 10.61, Y_cam: 6.12, Z_cam: 30.31)
Step 3 — Perspective Projection
4. Project onto 2D Canvas Screen (Pixels)
Objects farther away appear smaller through perspective division (dividing by depth $Z_{\text{cam}}$) and scaling by focal length $f = \text{fov} = 450$:
$$ X_{\text{screen}} = \frac{X_{\text{cam}} \cdot f}{Z_{\text{cam}}}, \quad Y_{\text{screen}} = \frac{Y_{\text{cam}} \cdot f}{Z_{\text{cam}}} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \begin{cases} x_{\text{pixel}} = \frac{W}{2} + X_{\text{screen}} \\ y_{\text{pixel}} = \frac{H}{2} - Y_{\text{screen}} \end{cases} $$
Live Calculation Breakdown:
// A. Relative screen coordinates from lens center:
X_screen = (X_cam * f) / Z_cam = (10.61 * 450) / 30.31 = 157.5 px
Y_screen = (Y_cam * f) / Z_cam = (6.12 * 450) / 30.31 = 90.8 px
// B. Map to Canvas pixel coordinates (Canvas origin (0,0) is at top-left):
pixel_x = (W / 2) + X_screen = (320) + (157.5) = 477.5 px
pixel_y = (H / 2) - Y_screen = (190) - (90.8) = 99.2 px
📐 The minus sign in pixel_y = (H / 2) - Y_screen is because Canvas Y points downward, while 3D camera Y points upward!
Linear Algebra Pipeline
5. The 4×4 View Matrix (LookAt Matrix)
In graphics APIs (OpenGL, DirectX, WebGL), the translation and basis rotation are combined into a single $4 \times 4$ affine transformation matrix:
$$ \mathbf{V} = \begin{bmatrix} r_x & r_y & r_z & -\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{camPos} \\ u_x & u_y & u_z & -\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{camPos} \\ f_x & f_y & f_z & -\mathbf{f} \cdot \mathbf{camPos} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} X_{\text{cam}} \\ Y_{\text{cam}} \\ Z_{\text{cam}} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{V} \begin{bmatrix} P_x \\ P_y \\ P_z \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
