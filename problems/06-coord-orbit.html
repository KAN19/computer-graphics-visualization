Core Concept — Mental Model
How Does "Orbiting" Actually Work?
• The 3D axes never move! Their vertices stay fixed at $[-5, +5]$ in world space.
• Instead, we place the camera (eye) on the surface of an invisible sphere of radius $R$ centered at the origin $(0, 0, 0)$, and force it to always stare at $(0, 0, 0)$ every frame.
• Moving the camera Left around the sphere while keeping its gaze locked on $(0, 0, 0)$ makes the coordinate system appear to spin Right on screen — creating the Orbit illusion in 4 pipeline stages below:
Stage 1 — Input Mapping & Polling
1. Convert Mouse, Wheel & Smooth Keys into $(R, \theta, \phi)$
Instead of moving $(x, y, z)$ along straight lines (which would change distance to the origin), user inputs control 3 spherical parameters inside the 60 FPS requestAnimationFrame loop:
// A. Smooth Key Polling (why we use Set instead of raw keydown):
keys.has("ArrowLeft" / "ArrowRight") → θ ∓= 1°/frame (no OS repeat lag!)
keys.has("ArrowUp" / "ArrowDown") → φ ±= 1°/frame
// B. Mouse DragArcball & Scroll Wheel:
mousemove (dx, dy) → θ += dx · 0.01,  φ −= dy · 0.01
wheel (deltaY) / +/- → R = clamp(R + ΔR, 3, 35)
⚠️ Why clamp $\phi \in (-89^\circ, +89^\circ)$? At $\phi = \pm 90^\circ$ (North/South pole), the camera sits directly on the $Z$ axis looking straight down $(0, 0, \mp 1)$, parallel to worldUp = (0, 0, 1). Their cross product becomes $(0, 0, 0)$, causing the camera to suddenly flip upside-down (Pole Flip / Gimbal Lock).
Stage 2 — Spherical to Cartesian
2. Project $(R, \theta, \phi)$ onto 3D Position $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$
Why do $x_0$ and $y_0$ multiply by $\cos\phi$? Think of a right triangle tilted up by elevation $\phi$:
1. Splitting hypotenuse $R$ vertically gives height $z_0 = R\sin\phi$ and a horizontal shadow on the XY floor of length $r_{xy} = R\cos\phi$.
2. Rotating that floor shadow $r_{xy}$ by horizontal angle $\theta$ splits it into $x_0$ and $y_0$:
$$ r_{xy} = R\cos\phi, \qquad \begin{cases} x_0 = r_{xy}\cos\theta = R \cos\phi \cos\theta \\ y_0 = r_{xy}\sin\theta = R \cos\phi \sin\theta \\ z_0 = R \sin\phi \end{cases} $$

Stage 3 — Turntable & Pause State
3. Add Auto-Rotation Around $Z$ Without Time Jumps
Using cos(time) directly would make the camera teleport when unpausing because wall-clock time keeps ticking while paused. Instead, we only add $\omega$ to an accumulator $\alpha$ when !paused, then rotate $(x_0, y_0)$ in 2D around $+Z$ (equivalent to $\theta_{\text{total}} = \theta + \alpha$):
$$ \begin{bmatrix} x_{\text{eye}} \\ y_{\text{eye}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\alpha & -\sin\alpha \\ \sin\alpha & \phantom{-}\cos\alpha \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_0 \\ y_0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad z_{\text{eye}} = z_0 $$
α = 0.0° (running) → θ_total = 45.0°
finalEye = (9.19, 9.19, 7.50)
Stage 4 — LookAt & MVP Matrix
4. Lock Camera Gaze onto $(0, 0, 0)$ via lookAt
Moving finalEye alone isn't enough — if the camera kept facing one fixed direction, the axes would slide off screen! Every frame, lookAt(finalEye, [0,0,0], [0,0,1]) rebuilds 3 orthonormal camera axes so the lens points straight back at the origin:
$$ \mathbf{f} = \frac{\mathbf{0} - \mathbf{eye}}{\|\mathbf{eye}\|}, \quad \mathbf{s} = \frac{\mathbf{f} \times \mathbf{up}}{\|\mathbf{f} \times \mathbf{up}\|}, \quad \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{s} \times \mathbf{f}, \quad \mathbf{MVP} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{persp}} \cdot \mathbf{V}_{\text{lookAt}} $$

Why Does the Red Triangle Disappear at Some Angles?
💡 To see this feature in action on the 3D canvas, check "Show Red XZ Triangle (01_triangle)" in the controls card at the top!
The triangle $(0,0,0) \to (3,0,0) \to (0,0,3)$ lies on the XZ plane with cullMode: "back" enabled. By the right-hand rule, its Front Face points toward $-Y$ — so when the camera rotates to the $+Y$ side (looking from behind), WebGPU hides its back side!
$$ \mathbf{n} = (3,0,0) \times (0,0,3) = (0, -9, 0) \parallel -\mathbf{Y} $$
